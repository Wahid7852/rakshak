how it works

How a person becomes a number, honestly

Every stage compares an employee to their own past, never to everyone else's. No single odd event is ever enough on its own.

01

Baseline

A fast and a slow running average per employee, per signal. Warms up over ~20 observations before it's trusted.

02

Deviation

Z-scores, novelty flags, and an unsupervised anomaly forest, all measured against that one person's history.

03

Decay

Risk fades on a 3-day half-life. A one-off event is forgotten if nothing follows it.

04

Verdict

Severity climbs only on sustained or corroborating signal, with a plain-language reason attached.

how we work

Real bugs this build caught on itself

Not process theater — four real defects a stress test surfaced during this build, and the actual fix, straight from docs/results.md and the engine source.

found

A fixed anomaly-score cutoff flagged 2 of 12 normal employees "critical" purely from the raw score floor varying by employee/feature-mix.

fixed

Trigger made self-relative: a z-score against that employee's own recent anomaly-score history, not a fixed global cutoff.

found

Variance-normalized drift z-score self-limits on a genuinely climbing series — the slow-drift insider scenario went undetected.

fixed

Added a non-normalized drift_ratio() for trending volume/transfer signals, kept drift_zscore() only for wandering ones like login hour.

found

A near-zero-but-positive contribution rate produced technically-correct but absurd projections like "12,756,548,269.1 days to critical."

fixed

365-day sanity horizon on the ETA projection — beyond that, it reports null instead of a nonsense number.

found

The collector agent persisted its read offset right after reading a file, before confirming the batch actually sent — a failed send got silently treated as delivered.

fixed

All-or-nothing flush per source; the offset only advances after a confirmed successful send.
